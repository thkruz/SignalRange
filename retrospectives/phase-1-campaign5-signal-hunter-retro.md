# Phase 1 — Campaign 5 (Signal Hunter) Geolocation Foundations Retro

## What worked

- **The opt-in patterns from Campaign 2 carried over cleanly.** Every new mechanic is additive and default-off: `emitter?` on interference events, `settings.geolocation?`, conditional Geolocation-tab registration (same gate as the Pass Schedule tab), and two additive `ConditionType`s. Campaigns 1–4 are byte-for-byte unchanged; the full 4563-test suite stayed green with no edits to existing tests.
- **Reusing `OrbitalSatellite` gave real orbital mechanics for free.** The SENTRY birds are SGP4-propagated from authored TLEs against the scenario clock — real ECI/velocity feed the TDOA/FDOA forward model. The console's live TDOA (~428 µs) and FDOA (~1077 Hz) at T+0 matched the standalone `author-tle-signal-hunter.mjs` validation exactly, confirming the app and the authoring script share one propagation path.
- **Separating the math into a pure `GeolocationService`** (no DOM/EventBus, like `pass-planner-service`) made the risk core unit-testable in isolation: exact recovery, realistic-noise recovery, ellipse tightening, LOP generation, degenerate-geometry guards. The measurement synthesis and the solver share the same ECEF forward model, so they're consistent by construction.
- **Driving the real app surfaced two bugs unit tests could not.** (1) The tab wired its DOM listeners in `addEventListeners_`, which `init_` calls *before* `this.dom_` is assigned — so the CAPTURE button did nothing. The fix (wire listeners after `dom_` is set) matches the existing PassScheduleTab pattern. (2) The original near-equatorial satellite geometry left the emitter *latitude* nearly unobservable from time-clustered captures, so the live fix landed ~100 km off even though every unit test passed (they spread captures over 15 min). Neither would have been caught without launching the app.

## What didn't

- **Geometry, not code, was the hardest problem.** Two closely-spaced, near-equatorial GEO satellites produce TDOA and FDOA gradients that both point mostly east–west, so latitude is weakly constrained unless the collection spans enough satellite motion. A near-zero-inclination pair (0.7°/1.2°) failed live; raising inclination to 3.0°/4.5° gave the birds N–S velocity, rotated the FDOA gradient, and made latitude observable from a single duty cycle. The 5°/6° option gave excellent latitude but collapsed the N/S mirror-ambiguity separation to ~cost 1.6 — a reminder that there's a real sweet spot. A throwaway `probe-geoloc-ambiguity.mjs` cost-surface scan found it; keeping that kind of probe around would help future geolocation scenarios.
- **The 25 km fix objective was too tight for the noise model.** Clustered captures give a large error ellipse (poor conditioning along one axis); the fix hovered right at 23–24 km and could randomly exceed 25. Loosened to 40 km — still rules out the ~150 km mirror and requires a genuine cross-fix, but tolerant of `Math.random()` noise and clustered operator behavior.
- **A noise-free unit test passed `sigma = 0`,** which divides by zero in the weighted cost and produced a NaN surface (a 303 km "fix"). Real scenarios always pass positive sigma, but the solver now floors sigma defensively and the test builds exact measurements with realistic weighting instead.
- **A 3-run background verification script hung** (likely a dev-server recompile mid-run) and produced no output, wasting a few cycles. The committed Playwright spec — which manages its own browser lifecycle and reuses the running server — was the right tool; the ad-hoc multi-run driver was not.

## What to change next time

- **Launch the app to validate geolocation geometry before authoring the scenario, not after.** The latitude-observability failure was invisible to unit tests. For any future TDOA/FDOA scenario, run the cost-surface probe (mirror separation + N/E cost rise from clustered captures) as part of TLE authoring, the way `author-tle` already validates pass timing.
- **Keep a geolocation-geometry probe as a checked-in dev tool**, alongside `author-tle-signal-hunter.mjs`, so the inclination/separation trade-off (latitude observability vs. mirror ambiguity) is reproducible for the next emitter site.
- **The E2E spec is mechanics-focused, not full-completion.** The capture step retries across the jammer's 60 s/45 s duty cycle rather than asserting on the first press. A full 6-objective completion spec (detect → characterize quiz → console → captures → fix → report) is the natural next artifact, but the duty-cycle timing makes it long; budget for it separately.
- **Consider surfacing the error ellipse conditioning to the player.** The fix tightens dramatically when captures are spread across several duty cycles (satellite motion improves geometry). The scenario text hints at this, but an in-console "geometry quality" indicator would make the teaching point explicit and reward good tradecraft.
- **Future difficulty tiers are cheap now that the plumbing exists:** reference-emitter calibration (remove ephemeris/clock bias), moving/pop-up jammers, multiple simultaneous emitters, and three-satellite fixes all build on `GeolocationService` + `settings.geolocation` without touching other campaigns.
